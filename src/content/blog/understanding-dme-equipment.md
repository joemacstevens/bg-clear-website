## What Is Durable Medical Equipment?

Durable Medical Equipment (DME) refers to medical devices and supplies prescribed by a healthcare provider for use in a patient's home. To qualify as DME under Medicare guidelines, an item must be durable enough for repeated use, primarily serve a medical purpose, and be appropriate for home use.

Common DME categories include mobility aids such as walkers, wheelchairs, and rollators; respiratory equipment like CPAP machines, nebulizers, and oxygen concentrators; patient monitoring devices including blood pressure monitors and pulse oximeters; and home hospital equipment such as adjustable beds and patient lifts.

## Why Compliance Matters

Every piece of DME distributed in the United States must meet specific regulatory requirements. The FDA oversees device safety and registration, while CMS sets the rules for Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement. Providers should verify that their DME supplier maintains proper accreditation — such as ACHC — and can furnish documentation for audits.

Working with a compliant distributor protects your practice from billing disputes, audit penalties, and patient safety concerns.
